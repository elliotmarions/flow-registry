Flow — Privacy Policy

Chrome extension “Flow” · Effective 1 September 2026 · Last updated 1 September 2026

The short version. Flow turns your music down while an AI assistant writes an answer, and turns it back up afterwards. Everything it needs to do that stays on your computer. Flow has no account, no server, no analytics, and no advertising. It does not collect, transmit, sell or share your personal data, and it never reads what you or an AI assistant write.

Who is responsible

Flow is made by Elliot Marions, an individual developer. Questions about this policy or about the extension can go to elliotmarions@gmail.com.

What Flow is for

Flow has a single purpose: to lower the volume of music playing in your browser at the moment an AI assistant’s answer arrives, and to raise it again when the answer is finished. Everything described below exists to serve that purpose and nothing else.

What Flow looks at

On the AI sites you allow

Flow needs to know when an answer is on its way and when it has finished. It reads three kinds of signal, all of them about timing:

  • That a network request to the service started and finished. Flow observes the address and the timing of the request the site makes when you send a message. It observes headers only. It never reads the body of a request or of a response, and Chrome’s extension API does not give it one unless it asks — Flow does not ask.
  • That a stop button appeared or disappeared on the page.
  • How much text has appeared in the element that holds the answer — the number of characters, never the characters themselves.

Flow does not read the text of your prompts, the text of the answers, page titles, conversation names, conversation history, account details, cookies, or anything you type. None of these are stored, and none are sent anywhere.

On the music sites you allow

Flow looks for audio and video elements on the page so that it can change their volume, and reads whether they are playing and how loud they are. It does not read what is playing, your listening history, playlists, or account details.

Everywhere else

Nothing. Flow only runs on the sites listed in its permissions, plus any site you add yourself on its settings page. It has no access to your other tabs, your browsing history, your bookmarks, your downloads, or your saved passwords.

What Flow stores, and where

All of it is stored by your browser, on your computer, using the extension storage Chrome provides. None of it is transmitted anywhere. None of it is readable by the developer or by any third party.

What Flow sends over the network

Two things, and neither carries information about you.

1. The service list

About twice a day, Flow requests a single file from https://elliotmarions.github.io/flow-registry/providers.json. This is how Flow learns that a site has changed its layout without you having to install an update. The request carries no parameters, no body, no identifier, and no information about your browsing, your settings, or your use of Flow.

Stated plainly for completeness: like any request to any website, this one reveals your IP address and browser user-agent string to the server that answers it, which is GitHub Pages, operated by GitHub, Inc. Flow neither reads nor records that; it is a property of making an HTTP request at all. GitHub’s handling of it is covered by GitHub’s privacy statement. If you would rather not have Flow make this request, the extension works without it, using the list bundled with the installed version.

2. The optional Windows companion app

If you install and pair the companion app, Flow opens a connection to 127.0.0.1 — the address that means “this computer”. It carries the pairing token you typed in, the extension’s version number, and volume commands: turn down, restore, pause, resume. Nothing about what is playing, what you are reading, or what you have typed. This traffic never leaves your machine and never reaches the internet. Pairing is entirely optional and off until you turn it on.

There is nothing else. Flow contacts no analytics service, no error reporting service, no advertising network, and no server belonging to the developer. There is no account to create and no identifier assigned to you or your installation.

The support report

Flow’s settings page has a button that writes a short diagnostic report and copies it to your clipboard, so you can paste it into an email if you ask for help. Nothing is sent automatically: the report is created only when you press the button, it goes only to your clipboard, and you decide whether to send it and to whom.

The report contains the extension version, your browser and operating system, your settings, the domains of the music and AI services in use, the counters described above, the version of the service list, the domains you have granted access to, and the last 20 log events. It contains no full web addresses, no page or conversation titles, no page content, and no identifiers. You can read all of it before you send it, which is the reason it is kept short.

What Flow never does

  • It does not sell or transfer your data to third parties.
  • It does not use your data for advertising, marketing, or profiling.
  • It does not use your data to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • It does not use your data for any purpose unrelated to turning your music down and back up again.
  • It does not track you across sites or build a profile of your activity.
  • It does not read, store or transmit the content of your conversations with an AI assistant.

Flow’s use of information received from Google APIs, and of all data it handles, adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your control over your data

  • See it. The settings page shows what Flow can reach, and the diagnostic button shows everything Flow has recorded.
  • Reduce it. You can withdraw access to any site from the settings page. Flow then stops seeing that site entirely.
  • Delete it. Removing the extension from Chrome deletes everything listed above. There is no copy anywhere else, because none was ever made.

Children

Flow is a volume control. It is not directed at children, and it collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If Flow ever begins handling data differently, this page will be updated before the change ships, and the date at the top will change with it. A change that would mean collecting or transmitting personal data would be announced in the extension itself, not only here.
